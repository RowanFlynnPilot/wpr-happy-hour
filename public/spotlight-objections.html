What bar owners say, and what to say back

Internal sales reference
Wausau Pilot & Review

They saySay thisThen ask
“I post my specials on Facebook. It’s free.” Keep posting. Facebook reaches your followers; the spotlight reaches our 41,000 newsletter subscribers, who open it for local news. Who would you like to see walk in who doesn’t follow you yet?
“It’s too expensive.” / “No budget.” It’s priced by the send, not a contract. Try one send on the night you most want to fill, then decide. Which night is slowest for you?
“Does email advertising even work?” I won’t promise you customers. I can show you 41,000 subscribers, and clicks to your site tagged so they show up in your own stats (as wausaupilotandreview / newsletter). Does your website have analytics? I’ll show you where to look.
“I don’t have time for ads.” / “My specials change all the time.” You don’t make anything: we build it from your specials and confirm them by phone. A change is one call or email. What’s running this week?
“We’re a restaurant, not a bar.” Food specials work the same way: Friday fish fry, prime rib night, an early-bird menu. What’s your best night for food?
“We don’t have a website.” Then the ad links to your Happy Hour Finder listing, with your specials and address. Where do people usually look up your hours?
“How is this different from your regular newsletter ad?” The banner runs all week in one edition, for any business ($300/week). The spotlight is by the send, built from your specials. Want every day? The banner may be the better buy. Every day, or just your special nights?
“Let me check with my partner.” Of course. I’ll send you the one-pager and a preview of the ad with your name on it. When should I follow up, and with whom?
“Will this get us a story?” No. Ads are labeled “Advertisement,” and the newsroom decides its coverage on its own. Check wording · Shereen Is there news coming up, like an anniversary or new owner? Send it to the newsroom.

Hard noes

  • A brewery or distributor paying for the bar’s ad. The bar buys its own. Wisconsin’s tied-house law limits what suppliers can give bars. Check · DOR The bar can still name their beer in its special.
  • “Bottomless,” all-you-can-drink or drinking-game copy. Some cities restrict these promotions. Stick to the special and its price.
  • Running before the call. An ad runs only from a confirmed listing. No confirmation, no ad.

Don’t sell it when

  • They have no steady specials. The ad would have nothing to show, so offer the banner instead.
  • They want to be in every edition all week. The $300/week banner fits that better.
  • They won’t commit to prices. The ad shows the prices you confirm, so it can’t run with guesses.

Before you hang up: send the preview with their name (swap in the bar’s name), then log any new objection in the call sheet’s Call notes column.
