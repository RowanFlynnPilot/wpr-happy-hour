What bar owners say, and what to say back

Promise only what you can show them: the audience, the ad, and their own clicks.

  1. I post my specials on Facebook. It’s free.”

    Keep posting. Facebook reaches your followers. We reach 41,000 subscribers.

    Ask: Who would you like to see walk in who doesn’t follow you yet?

  2. It’s too expensive. There’s no budget.”

    Priced by the send, not a contract. Try it on the night you most want to fill.

    Ask: Which night is slowest for you?

  3. Does email advertising even work?”

    I won’t promise customers. I can show you 41,000 subscribers, and the clicks in your own site stats (tagged “wausaupilotandreview / newsletter”).

    Ask: Does your website have analytics? I’ll show you where to look.

  4. No time for ads. My specials keep changing.”

    Nothing to make. We build it from your specials, and a change is one call.

    Ask: What’s running this week?

  5. We’re a restaurant, not a bar.”

    Food specials work the same: Friday fish fry, prime rib night, early-bird menus.

    Ask: What’s your best night for food?

  6. We don’t have a website.”

    Then the ad links to your Happy Hour Finder listing, with specials and address.

    Ask: Where do people usually look up your hours?

  7. How’s this different from your regular ads?”

    The banner runs all week in one edition, at $300 a week. The spotlight is by the send, built from your specials. Want every day? The banner may fit better.

    Ask: Every day, or just your special nights?

  8. Let me check with my partner.”

    Of course. I’ll send the one-pager and a preview with your name on it.

    Ask: When should I follow up, and with whom?

  9. Will this get us a story?”

    No. Ads are labeled “Advertisement,” and the newsroom decides coverage on its own.Check wording with Shereen

    Ask: Got news coming up, like an anniversary? Send it to the newsroom.

Hard noes

  • A brewery or distributor paying for it. The bar buys its own ad; Wisconsin’s tied-house law limits what suppliers give bars.Check with DOR Naming their beer in the special is fine.
  • “Bottomless” or drinking-game copy. Some cities restrict these. Stick to the special and its price.
  • An ad before the call. It runs only from a confirmed listing.

Don’t sell it when

  • No steady specials. The ad would have nothing to show; offer the banner.
  • They want every edition, all week. The $300-a-week banner fits that better.
  • No firm prices. The ad shows the prices you confirm; it can’t run on guesses.

Before you hang up: send the preview with their name, and log any new objection in the call sheet’s Call notes column.
